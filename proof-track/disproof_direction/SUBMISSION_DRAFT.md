## Description (paste into the form)

**Title: Exhaustive length-capped search of the 550 open Miller–Schupp presentations: peak-length
lower bounds and replayable class certificates (partial result, disproof direction).**

**What is established.**
We work in the official rank-2 elementary move graph (`ac-r2-v1`: 14 moves, exact freely reduced
words). Let `G_L` be the subgraph on presentations of total relator length ≤ L. Let Σ be the group of
order 16 generated by the signed permutations of {x, y} and the swap of the two relators. Every element
of Σ is an automorphism of `G_L`. The component of the standard presentation `(x,y)` is Σ-invariant for
L ≥ 3. So if P is AC-equivalent to σ(Q) for some σ in Σ, then P is AC-trivial if and only if Q is
(and likewise for stable AC).

1. **(Computer-verified; peak-length lower bounds.)** For each of the 550 `open` presentations
   `MS(n,w) = <x,y | x^-1 y^n x = y^{n+1}, x = w>` of the official MS-1190 list, and for AK(3), we
   exhausted the Σ-quotient of its connected component in `G_C`, with C = 25 (C = 26 for the 38
   instances in the two MS(2) length-14 blocks). None of these components contains `(x,y)`.
   * Hence **every official-move AC-trivialisation of any open MS instance, or of AK(3), passes
     through a presentation of total relator length ≥ 26**, and ≥ 27 for the two MS(2) length-14
     blocks.
   * For AK(3), the cap-25 component has 729,472,671 Σ-orbits. It contains exactly 34 of the open MS
     instances.
2. **(Certified by explicit paths.)** We give 556 Σ-certificates and 2,752 exact certificates. All
   were replayed with the official verifier's `apply_move`, with 0 failures. They show:
   * the 550 open instances fall into **at most 207 Σ-classes**. Members of a class are simultaneously
     AC-trivial or not (and likewise for stable AC), so there are at most 207 genuinely different
     counterexample candidates in this family;
   * they fall into **at most 431 exact AC classes**;
   * all 34 instances labelled `13_1` are AC-equivalent to AK(3), with peak ≤ 20;
   * MS(2, x^-2y^-1x^2y^-1) ~ MS(2, yx^2yx^-2) (90 moves, peak 23) and MS(2, x^-2y^-1x^2y) ~
     MS(2, yx^2y^-1x^-2) (40 moves, peak 23). Carreras (arXiv:2607.23611) certified these pairs with
     peaks 33 and 27.

   No exact class mixes two Two-Hump labels (Fagan et al. 2026). The Σ-classes group the 261 labels
   into 133 groups, the same count as Carreras's σ-merge programme.
3. **(Computer-verified; barriers.)** The cap-26 components of the two MS(2) length-14 blocks contain
   neither AK(3) nor each other, even up to Σ. So the AC-equivalences to AK(3) stated without proof by
   Shehper et al. (2024) need intermediate presentations of **total length ≥ 27** in the official
   elementary move graph. This is the elementary-graph counterpart of Carreras's GS-graph bound; the
   two are logically independent. Separately, 47 of the 261 Two-Hump labels split across our
   Σ-classes at caps 24–25.
4. **(Negative.)** The finite-quotient AC-orbit invariant separates nothing. We tested S3, A4, S4, D5,
   F20 and A5 exhaustively, and S5, PSL(2,7), A6 and PSL(2,11) on samples, against AK(3), all 550
   instances and 424 controls. In every group tested, all generating pairs form one AC_G component,
   consistent with Borovik–Lubotzky–Myasnikov (arXiv:1103.1295).

**What remains open.** Everything that matters for a disproof. Length-capped non-reachability is **not**
evidence of the strength a disproof needs:
* Bridson (arXiv:1504.04187) and Lishak (arXiv:1504.00418) exhibit balanced presentations whose
  trivialisations need super-exponentially many moves, and hence very large intermediate lengths;
* Lisitsa found an MS trivialisation of 8,634 steps.

AK(3) is known to be stably AC-trivial (Shehper et al. 2024; Lisitsa, arXiv:2501.18601), so it is
open only for unstable AC. Our bounds are for the elementary, exact-word graph. They do not transfer to
cyclic-word or arbitrary-conjugator formulations. The largest components are verified by code
cross-validation, not by shipped closure certificates.

**Scope.** Rank 2, non-stable AC only. The Σ-reduction applies equally to stable AC-triviality.

**Prior work used, and how.**
* Official competition data and verifier (SAIR / Caltech): MS-1190 metadata, move semantics, and
  replay.
* Miller–Schupp (1999): the family.
* Akbulut–Kirby (1985): AK(n).
* Havas–Ramsay (2003) and Miasnikov–Myasnikov (arXiv:math/0304305): the length-12/13 classification.
* Shehper et al. (arXiv:2408.15332): MS benchmark, AK(3) stably trivial, and unpublished equivalence
  claims.
* Fagan et al., "Two-Hump" (arXiv:2606.21611): the `reported_class` labels. We compare with them; we
  do not use them as input.
* Carreras (arXiv:2607.23611): the length-14 certificates, GS bounds and σ-merge count. We compare
  with them only.
* Panteleev–Ushakov (arXiv:1609.00325), Bridson, Lishak, Borovik–Lubotzky–Myasnikov, Lisitsa: cited
  for context and limitations.

All code, certificates and data are ours. The code is C++ BFS in two independent algorithms plus
Python replay, and was written with AI assistance under human direction. Every claim rests on
replayable certificates or reproducible exhaustive runs.
